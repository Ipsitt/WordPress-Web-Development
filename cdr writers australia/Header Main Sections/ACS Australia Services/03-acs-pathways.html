Which ACS assessment pathway fits you: General Skills, Post Australian Study, RPL or Qualification Only

ACS asks you to pick one pathway before you apply, and it gives no pre-assessment advice, so decide carefully.

General Skills

ACS calls this its most common pathway, for experienced ICT, data science and cyber security professionals. It checks the ICT content of your qualification, its relevance to your ANZSCO occupation, and your professional work experience.

Post Australian Study

For graduates with an Australian bachelor degree or higher and a closely related ICT or data science major. You need one year (365 days) of relevant work after graduating, or a completed ACS Professional Year.

Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)

For people with substantial ICT, data science or cyber security experience but no relevant tertiary qualification. It does not assess any qualification and relies on two project reports.

Qualification Only

Assesses your qualification alone, with no work experience evidence. ACS lists it for the Temporary Graduate 485 (Post-Vocational Education Work stream), which needs an Australian diploma or associate degree, and for enrolment in the Professional Year.

ICT major or minor decides your experience requirement. For a bachelor degree or higher under General Skills, ACS lists 2 years in the last 10 (or 4 years at any time) for a closely related ICT major, and 5 years in the last 10 (or 6 years at any time) for a closely related ICT minor. If the major is not closely related, the figures are 4 years for a major and 6 years for a minor. ACS says the classification is only determined at formal assessment, so no one can promise it in advance.

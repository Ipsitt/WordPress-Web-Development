AIMS skills assessment FAQs: fees, processing time, English and exams

Straight answers to what medical laboratory professionals search before they apply.

What is an AIMS skills assessment?
It is the migration skills assessment run by the Australian Institute of Medical and Clinical Scientists (AIMS). A committee assesses your qualifications and pathology laboratory experience against Australian standards for one of its four assessed occupations.
Which occupations does AIMS assess for migration?
AIMS assesses Medical Laboratory Scientist (ANZSCO 234611), Medical Laboratory Scientist with the IVF Embryologist specialisation (234611), Medical Laboratory Technician (311213) and Pathology Collector or Phlebotomist (311216).
How much does an AIMS skills assessment cost?
The assessment fee is AUD 900 (excluding GST) if your address is outside Australia and AUD 990 (including 10% GST) if it is in Australia. A professional examination costs a further AUD 800 or AUD 880. Fees can change, so check AIMS's fee page before paying.
How long does an AIMS skills assessment take?
AIMS's website says it aims to finish assessments within 16 weeks of receiving them, while its occupation guidelines state six months. Document verification can take up to six months, and AIMS offers no express service.
Is AIMS membership enough for migration?
No. AIMS membership cannot be used for migration purposes. You can apply for membership alongside a migration assessment, but only the skills assessment counts for your visa application.
What are the AIMS English language requirements?
From 12 March 2026, AIMS no longer lists required scores in its forms, so check the Department of Home Affairs website for current scores. All four skills must come from one sitting, results with a One Skill Retake component are not accepted, and the report must reach AIMS within three years of the test date.
Do I have to sit the AIMS professional examination?
Not always. Medical Laboratory Scientists with an AIMS accredited degree are classified without the exam. Other routes need a document assessment first and then the Professional Examination or the Special Professional Examination, held in March and September.
What is the ANZSCO code for a Medical Laboratory Scientist?
The ANZSCO code for Medical Laboratory Scientist is 234611, and the IVF Embryologist specialisation uses the same code. Medical Laboratory Technician is 311213 and Pathology Collector or Phlebotomist is 311216.
How much experience does AIMS require?
It depends on the route. Option 2 and Option 3 for Medical Laboratory Scientists need two years of post-degree experience in a medical pathology laboratory, one of them in the last five years. Medical Laboratory Technicians and Pathology Collectors also need two years within the five years before applying, unless the technician route uses New Zealand registration.
How long is an AIMS skills assessment valid?
An AIMS assessment is valid for three years from the date of the original Stage 1 skills assessment results letter.
Can I appeal an AIMS skills assessment outcome?
Yes. You can request a free review by email within one month of receiving your Stage 1 results letter. If you still disagree, you can appeal by email within one month of the review result, and an appeal fee applies.
Does a positive AIMS skills assessment guarantee a visa?
No. AIMS cannot give migration advice, and a positive assessment is only one part of a skilled visa application. The Department of Home Affairs or a registered migration agent can advise on visas.

Why a compliant employment reference letter is crucial

Assessors judge your experience against fixed criteria. These four checks decide whether your employment counts.

Minimum working hours

Many authorities, including VETASSESS and Engineers Australia, count only paid work of at least 20 hours a week. Your letter has to state your weekly hours clearly, for every role.

Paid work below the 20-hour line is not counted.

ANZSCO alignment

Your day-to-day tasks must match the description of the occupation you are nominating. Assessors compare your letter with it line by line, so vague or unrelated wording cannot be counted.

  • Plans and delivers campaigns
  • Manages client accounts
  • Analyses market research
  • Occupation task 1
  • Occupation task 2
  • Occupation task 3

Each duty in your letter should map to the occupation, in your own wording.

Verified employment dates

Exact start and end dates, written DD/MM/YYYY, let the assessor build a continuous timeline and work out how much experience counts. Unclear dates can leave a period unrecognised.

Continuous, exact dates leave no unexplained gap for the assessor.

Terms and remuneration

Employment status (full-time, part-time, contract or casual), salary and benefits must agree with your payslips, tax records or bank statements.

Letter saysSalary $85,000, full-time
Payslip shows$85,000 a year, 38 hrs

When the letter and the pay records agree, the claim is easy to verify.

Why details matter: a letter with missing items, imprecise wording or no valid signature can trigger a request for information (RFI) or leave a period of employment unrecognised. Some assessors, such as the Australian Computer Society, will not assess a letter that lacks a required item.

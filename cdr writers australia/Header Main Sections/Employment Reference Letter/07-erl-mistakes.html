Common Employment Reference Letter Mistakes that trigger an RFI

Most requests for information come from the same few gaps.

Duties copied from the ANZSCO

Assessors can spot wording lifted from the occupation guide or another applicant's letter, and ACS will not accept it.

Tap for the fix
The fix

Write duties from what you actually did, in your own role terms, and then align them to the occupation.

Hours and status left out

Without weekly hours and employment type, the assessor cannot tell whether the work meets the 20-hour minimum.

Tap for the fix
The fix

State full-time, part-time, casual or contract, plus minimum paid hours per week, for every role.

Vague or overlapping dates

Dates such as "2019 to 2022" or periods that overlap between employers make the timeline impossible to verify.

Tap for the fix
The fix

Use DD/MM/YYYY for each start and end date, and give each position its own dates.

No letterhead, signature or work email

A letter that looks unofficial, is unsigned or lists a personal email address cannot be verified.

Tap for the fix
The fix

Use company letterhead, an authorised signature and date, and the signatory's work email and phone.

Letter and records disagree

A salary, title or date in the letter that does not match your payslips, CV or tax records raises doubt.

Tap for the fix
The fix

Check every detail against your payslips, tax records, bank statements and CV before you submit.

Wrong person or company signs

For agency or contract work, the host company where you did the work is usually who should confirm your duties.

Tap for the fix
The fix

Get the reference from the company you worked at, and confirm the signer's role in the business.

P.Eng Canadian experience requirement: six myths, checked against the regulators

Myth

You need a year of Canadian work experience.

Not any more. PEO does not require Canadian experience, and EGBC has not for many years. APEGA removed that need from 18 July 2026 through Canadian environment equivalencies.

Myth

Two years of experience is enough in Ontario.

It is the minimum, not the norm. PEO's two-year floor started on 1 July 2026, but it says most applicants need three to four years to show all 34 competencies.

Myth

Overseas experience automatically counts.

No. EGBC states that international experience does not automatically meet the Canadian environment competencies. You must show equivalence.

Myth

Co-op always counts toward the minimum.

It depends on the regulator. PEO does not count undergraduate co-op toward its two years, while EGBC counts up to one supervised pre-graduation year.

Myth

The NPPE is part of the competency assessment.

They are separate. The NPPE is an exam on professionalism, law and ethics. Your CBA is judged on your written work examples.

Myth

A higher self-rating is safer.

Rate honestly. Validators score independently, and assessors make the final call. EGBC also warns you not to rate yourself below the required minimum.

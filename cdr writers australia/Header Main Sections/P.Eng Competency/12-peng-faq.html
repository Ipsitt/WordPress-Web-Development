EGBC P.Eng competency FAQs: experience, validators, NPPE and timelines

Straight answers to what engineers search before they start their P.Eng competency report.

What is the P.Eng competency assessment?
It is the competency-based assessment (CBA) that Engineers and Geoscientists British Columbia (EGBC) uses to judge your work experience. You write work examples, rate yourself, and validators who observed your work confirm and score them.
How many competencies are in the EGBC P.Eng competency assessment?
EGBC assesses 34 competencies in seven categories. Eight of them are the Canadian environment competencies, each with its own minimum rating.
How long does it take to get a P.Eng with EGBC?
EGBC requires a minimum of four years of engineering experience. Once your application is complete, EGBC's review takes about six months, so allow for validator and document delays on top of that.
Do I need Canadian experience to get a P.Eng in BC?
No, EGBC does not require Canadian work experience. You must still meet the eight Canadian environment competencies, and international experience does not count automatically, so explain how your overseas work was equivalent.
What experience counts toward EGBC's four-year minimum?
Up to one year of supervised pre-graduation or co-op experience can count. Postgraduate study can also earn credit, up to one year for a thesis master's degree and two years for a PhD.
What score do I need in each EGBC competency category?
Most categories need a minimum average of 3 out of 5, while Project and Financial Management and Social, Economic, Environmental and Sustainability need 2. Each competency is rated from 0 to 5, and EGBC does not expect you to work at an advanced level of 4 or 5.
How many validators do I need for an EGBC application?
EGBC requires at least four validators who together cover at least four years of your experience. One of them must be a supervisor in the same or a related discipline.
How do I write an EGBC competency example?
Use the Situation, Action, Outcome structure in the first person. Describe the context briefly, spend most of your words on your own actions and judgements, and explain the result with numbers where possible. Avoid vague phrases such as participated in, and use recent examples where you can.
What is the NPPE and when do I get my result?
The National Professional Practice Examination (NPPE) tests professionalism, law and ethics, and it is separate from your competency assessment. EGBC says results appear on the status portal about three to four weeks after the exam, and that a booked proctoring session cannot be cancelled or deferred.
What is the difference between APEGBC and EGBC?
There is no difference in the regulator. APEGBC is the old abbreviation for the Association of Professional Engineers and Geoscientists of BC, and Engineers and Geoscientists British Columbia (EGBC) is the name it adopted for everyday use in August 2017. The association's legal name is still the longer Association of Professional Engineers and Geoscientists of the Province of British Columbia, but applicants deal with EGBC, so use EGBC when you search or follow its guidance.
Can I use AI for my EGBC competency examples?
EGBC states that using AI to create or embellish workplace examples is strictly prohibited and can lead to disciplinary action. Treat any help as guidance on structure and clarity.

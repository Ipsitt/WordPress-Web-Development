A CDR is a technical document submitted to Engineers Australia comprising three Career Episodes, a Summary Statement, and a CPD list. It's required for overseas-trained engineers applying for Australian skilled migration visas like Subclass 189, 190, or 491.
Three Career Episodes are required, each describing a separate engineering project where you personally contributed. Each episode should be 1,000–2,500 words, written in first-person.
The standard Migration Skills Assessment (CDR pathway) fee is approximately AUD 907.50. Fees are subject to change, always verify on the Engineers Australia official website before applying.
Standard CDR assessments typically take 8–12 weeks. Complex cases or incomplete submissions may take longer. Submit well ahead of your visa deadline.
Engineers Australia does not publish official acceptance rates, but rejection rates are high for first-time applicants often due to plagiarism, weak personal contribution, or misaligned Summary Statements.
You can resubmit after addressing the reasons cited in the rejection notice. The full assessment fee applies again. Professional CDR review before resubmission is strongly recommended.
Overseas-trained engineers whose qualifications are not accredited under international accords (Washington, Sydney, or Dublin Accord) must submit a CDR to qualify for a positive skills assessment for Australian PR.
The top reasons are: plagiarized content, using "we" instead of "I", episodes describing the same project, weak Summary Statement mapping, and career episodes that lack technical depth.
